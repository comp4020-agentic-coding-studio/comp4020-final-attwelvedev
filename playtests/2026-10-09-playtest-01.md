- **Date:** 2026-10-09
- **Participants**: myself Aaron, my partner Hanna, and friend April; all have some gaming experience, but not extensive
- **Setting**: same room
- **Devices**: all laptops; we all had headphones; we didn't speak aloud
- **Consent**: obtained from all participants
- **State of repo**: [`1b34024`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-attwelvedev/commit/1b340240a96ab613ecdf16d865887a11807374eb); this corresponds to the implementation of the plans up to and including [2026-10-08-sensory-heist-06-bots-disconnects.md](../plans/2026-10-08-sensory-heist-06-bots-disconnects.md).

## What happened

This was the first informal playtest of my game with my friends and myself. We attempted all rooms, and it lasted about one hour. The roles were automatically determined and rotated by the game, depending on our initial lobby joining order.

| Role | Room 1 | Room 2 | Room 3 |
| -- | -- | -- | -- |
| Can't see | Hanna | Aaron | April |
| Can't hear | April | Hanna | Aaron |
| Can't speak | Aaron | April | Hanna |

I had already discussed the game concept at a high level with Hanna, but she was unaware of the specific game mechanics. April had no prior knowledge of the game, nor was given an explanation of what the game is about.

The lobby creation and sharing process was quick and easy. Hanna was impressed that when she created the lobby, it immediately showed up on my screen in the "Open lobbies" list. April was confused by the role assignment screen not understanding what any of the "Say", "Sound, "Show" meant. I explained that once she started playing, it would make sense.

When the first room started, she still was confused about the objective, not knowing about what to do. She started going off into the map without helping Hanna who couldn't see, since I couldn't speak. Hanna and I found this amusing, bursting out laughing, before April finally realised she needed to help Hanna. We were still amused by the back-and-forth process between April sending messages to Hanna, and Hanna moving carefully. With my role being unable to speak, I couldn't really help in this first round, so I just sent funny soundboard sounds reacting to the situation, which only Hanna could hear, and she found it funny. I did try to send sounds of approval when Hanna was doing something right, and disapproval when she was going in the wrong direction; but overall, it couldn't add anything more than what April was saying. We all cheered and felt satisfied completing the first level, which we found relatively easy. Overall, despite receiving no explanations, they figured out the mechanics of pushing the crate, the simultaneity requirement of standing on the door plates to open the door, picking up the loot, and the end zone.

Moving on to the second room, I couldn't see, so Hanna had to help me get past the hazards: the camera, laser, and guard. The timing to get past the hazards proved difficult, but was more hilarious than annoying. After I managed to get past the camera, or laser, at times, the other two would randomly get caught too, despite having vision; which was even more hilarious when contrasted to me. Again, the new hazards on this level were intuitive to learn. Hanna did remark at first she didn't know what the camera was, but did quickly realise. But like the last room, the can't speak role didn't feel like it was directly useful to helping the others. April just wandered off and got to the end, waiting for Hanna and I to make our way there. Like myself when I played can't speak, she just played funny sounds while waiting. We spent a bit more time compared to the last room to complete this room.

Lastly, the third and last room; this room was quite problematic. As I couldn't hear, and Hanna couldn't speak, I had to help April move. She actually managed to follow my instructions pretty well, but it all unravelled at the dark area. I couldn't hear, so I couldn't time when the guard would be looking away and thus when it was safe to go. Hanna was able to listen to the guard's footsteps and April was confused about when to go despite being able to hear, which I assume means she probably wanted a more concrete sign, given she couldn't see. So Hanna and I agreed that when she determines it was safe to go, she would use the air horn, and for moving downwards, she would use the car horn. This was the exact shared-language behaviour and strategy I had hoped I designed for. I couldn't hear these sounds, but I could see them entering the darkness, so I was able to safely follow them in. But getting to the lasers, there was no way for me to receive any communication since my role meant I couldn't hear, and now, in the darkness, apart from myself, I couldn't see anything; my teammates, stamps, faces. The other two could make it to the other side of the darkness, but I was essentially stranded, and if I wanted to make it through, then I would have to rely on chance to make it past the lasers. This was the first time we all needed to communicate a strategy that included everyone, but we were limited by the design of the dark area which made it  impossible for the can't hear player to strategically make it past. When Hanna reached the other side, she also managed to unlock the door by sequentially stepping on the plates in the given order, but all by herself; which is not intended design. Due to the dark area being strategically impossible to solve, we gave up trying to completing this room.

After the gameplay, I asked for general feedback from both. April said it was really fun and the concept was good and Hanna agreed. I asked for suggestions for improvements, and Hanna raised a good point: we all had some gaming experience, but there may be some players who don't understand the game knowledge that this game assumes, so it may be a good idea to have a tutorial which teaches beginners what each game object is, such as the plates needing all players standing on them simultaneously to open the door, or where to go to complete the room. Hanna and April were both a bit confused about the three comm sheet buttons "Say", "Sound", and "Show". Even I, who developed this game, felt that the buttons weren't clear in their meaning, and also like each actual comm action was being unnecessary hidden and grouped in those buttons. Using the comms was doable and simple to figure out, but didn't feel optimally intuitive, especially when I'm focused on the gameplay and trying to help my teammates. Further discussing the comms, Hanna didn't like how the opened comm sheets obscured the map, which blocked her vision of where she was moving.

I pressed further on the visuals, because my goal is to make a unique and eye-catching game. Hanna said the UI was good, with pleasant, easy-on-the-eye colours and fonts. She also said nothing ever looked ugly, in the room design, or the rest of the website. She commended the minimalist, simple design. She did say the players or room itself could be larger to fit the screen, as the players and their face reactions felt small.

## What can be brought forward

What is currently working well includes the game concept itself and its gameplay implications, the visuals, the setup flow, and the realtime updates (e.g., the lobby opening instantly).

| Finding | Action | Where it lands |
| -- | -- | -- |
| "Say", "Sound", "Show" framing is confusing to understand | Likely step away from this framing, and just mention the actual role-specific actions themselves which players are more likely to be familiar with; try new layout/s (TBC) | open question, playtest 2 |
| Comm sheets are a bit clunky to use intuitively, and obscure the map | Possibly remove the explicit categories, make the actions easier to trigger quickly, potentially in a circle around the map instead, or something (TBC) | TBD |
| In-game elements are slightly confusing to figure out | A tutorial/explanation in `README.md`, or toggle-able explanations embedded in the map in the beginning rooms; additionally, clearer visual design for the game elements (TBC) | TBD |
| Can't speak doesn't have a prominent role | TBC, a check that removing can't speak makes the rooms unsolvable | TBD |
| Dark area needs to be strategically possible for the can't hear role | Have visual clues that make realistic and practical sense, like teammate outlines emulating feeling in real life, or stamps and faces visible in the dark; a check that no flip or combination leaves a role with no input | `/spec` |
| Sequential door plates need to require all players | Require one player per plate; double-check solver requiring three players | `/spec` |
