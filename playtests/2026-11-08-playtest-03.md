- **Date:** 2026-11-08 (close to midnight, played until 2026-11-09)
- **Participants**: myself Aaron, my partner Hanna; all have some gaming experience, but not extensive
- **Setting**: same room
- **Devices**: all laptops; both had headphones; we spoke aloud
- **Consent**: obtained from all participants
- **State of repo**: [`32739e8`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-attwelvedev/commit/32739e80ae3a274a24aca11b4bbbbc3a9db36b1c); this corresponds to the implementation of the plans up to and including [2026-11-07-playtests-02-regression.md](../plans/2026-11-07-playtests-02-regression.md).

## What happened

This was the second informal playtest of my game with my partner. The two of us played in one lobby, with a bot in the third seat. We attempted all rooms, and it lasted about 1.5-2 hours. The roles were automatically determined and rotated by the game, depending on our initial lobby joining order.

Hanna was already aware of the game and its mechanics from [the first playtest](2026-10-09-playtest-01.md). I also did briefly show her the new escape room-like mechanics implemented about 1 week before this playtest, but I did not explain the solutions to the rooms.

Hanna said:

- The gradient effect on the figures makes them stand out more, looks slightly 3D; overall looks better
- Start button positioned better and more clearly on the role assignment screen, instead of at the bottom from before

She found that:

- Earplugs was confused for headphones in the setup, when read quickly
  - I had to correct her
- She didn't know when hand is held as can't see; unclear
  - Should be a notification/toast when someone is holding hand
  - Or, see a blurry outline of the other player, with the link, simulating the feeling of holding hands

I found that:

- For sighted players, the hand holding label appears only after hand holding, so for new players, they might not know they can hold hands if they don't see the action hint in the corner
  - Should have a label that follows the player when they are in hand holding radius
- Checkpoints don't have a label to explain what they are

We found that:

- Bot occasionally keeps sending callouts or talking for the finished game when in the next role assignment screen
- Comms wheel doesn't show the 1 2 3 4 top tabs when the window height is low
- The edge chips for faces don't show in the right direction when other player isn't in LoS
  - Observed when Hanna tried to send me faces through a wall, and the edge chip appeared in the top-right corner, face appearing cut-off the screen

Hanna said she:

- Likes the side jobs, braille
- Action hint is useful for can't see

I thought that:

- Can't speak seems to dominate the keypad
  - They can simply go to the vent, listen, then go to the keypad and solve it
  - Can't hear can't help very much
  - Can't see only helps a bit, when it requires braille

Hanna found that:

- It was a struggle going through door openings
  - I didn't have such issue, but perhaps I'm more familiar with the game
- Bots spamming direction callouts for the can't see player is annoying
  - She didn't say anything much, just seemed a bit annoyed and went to settings to turn the spoken lines off
- In room 6, when the bot was can't hear, they used X marks to help out on the lights
  - But she didn't understand this was from the bot, and thought it was just the design of the level
  - And “X” is unclear about whether to press it or avoid pressing it
- Also in room 6, she thought the braille next to the radio would be useful in solving it or providing a clue
  - But the radio had a bug where it could be solved by just spamming the dials until it had a tick mark
  - I had to tell her it was irrelevant, but she was confused
  - This bug was detected by the agent later on during a code review, and it fixed it
    - I told it to not have a "solved" state, where the braille provides the correct radio band to listen to
    - Fixed in: [`2a6fdee`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-final-attwelvedev/commit/2a6fdeeb87b017e2a6eb5316a450b815103db672)
- Room 7 bot doesn't help with side job
- Hard to communicate with bots to tell them what to do
  - E.g., when can't see has a side job, can't speak bot can't help with the side job
  - The can't see human tries to communicate with can't speak, but because it's a bot, it does nothing
  - It also doesn't understand the other comms, because humans invent meaning for them that bots can't possibly know
  - If the other human is can't hear, they can't know what can't see is saying; faces and marks are limited in what they can express
  - Thus it's hard for the can't see player
  - Could bots understand natural language to communicate more easily?

We:

- Improvised and used numbering system for faces to communicate numbers
  - As can't hear, I took the lead and suggested we use the comm wheel and count from the top, clockwise, numbering each face
  - When she was can't see, she would follow this system to read braille plates to me
  - At first, she was a bit confused by the hash symbol, but learnt quickly to ignore it
    - About this, I found that the hash is always in the first position, so it is very predictable
    - Maybe it can be changed so that players don't just memorise

## What can be brought forward

What is working well: the gradient figures and the new Start position (both noticed unprompted), side jobs and braille, and the action hint for Can't see. Most of all, we invented a language. Counting the wheel's faces clockwise to send numbers is the game's pitch happening, and it came from people, not the game. Roles are still not balanced on the keypad, and the bot is the weakest teammate.
