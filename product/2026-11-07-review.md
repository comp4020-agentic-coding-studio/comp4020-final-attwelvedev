## 3. First five minutes

Observed in the walk unless marked.

- **No goal in room 1** (basic). After the reveal, each sighted player sees a corridor with one chip, "Plate: one person on each opens the door". Nothing says *get everyone to the exit*. The exit's label ("Exit: everyone on it ends the room") only appears when the exit comes into sight. Can't see sees only "No map. Listen." Playtest 1's April "was confused about the objective" with the old game too. This was never fixed, only re-labelled. (`07-game15s-1.png`, `src/client/labels.ts`)
- **"A heist for three" lands you in "Players 1 of 6" and bots** (basic). Someone who starts a team alone is told "Share the code to fill seats; bots fill the rest", and gets nine rooms listed as "Ana · bot · bot". For Priya-type users this is the moment the game turns into a bot game. (`03b-lobby-alone-full-desk.png`)
- **Start is below the fold on a laptop** (basic). At 1280×800 the host's "Start (2 bots fill)" sits under the setup form at about y=865. The first screen is a giant code, the team name, setup toggles and nine rooms. A host may not see how to begin. (`03-lobby-alone-desk.png` vs `03b-…full`)
- **The reveal is a table of method names** (performance). "You can send: talk, faces · You get: talk, clips", with a "can't" underlined under Bo. It's better than playtest 1's "Say/Sound/Show", but it still teaches the *system* before the *goal*. Can't hear's reveal lists four methods and eight receivers. (`05-reveal-1.png`)
- **The phone Can't speak seat: "Wheel", "OK", "Hold hands"** (performance). There's a joystick, an "OK" quick-send (to whom?), a "Wheel" button, and a "Can't talk" chip that reads like an error. The phone has no help line like the desktop's "WASD or arrows move · Space acts". (`07-game15s-2.png`)
- **Microphone blocked line on every desktop seat** (performance). "Microphone blocked: voice is off" sits in the HUD before anyone has been asked to talk, which reads like something is broken. (Observed in headless, where the mic is denied. A real first-timer sees a permission prompt, then this line if they decline.)
- **Brute-forcing a safe by spamming Act** (performance, inferred from the backlog). Partly addressed by bp-03's 3 s jam (`e440947`).
- **Nine rooms, no "short game"** (performance). There's no way to promise a group "20 minutes". Lobby "Rooms and who plays" lists all nine.

What works, and should be protected: the landing page's three live bot feeds explain the concept without words, the "That's on purpose" line, "Learn the sounds", the Copy/QR/open-lobby flow, and the dark frame for Can't see.

## 4. PRFAQ

### Press release

**Sensory heist lets three friends pull off a heist when one can't see, one can't hear and one can't speak**

*A free browser party game for three, on laptops or phones. No account, no download. Each person is missing a sense, so the team has to invent its own way of talking.*

CANBERRA, 9 November 2026: Sensory heist is a co-op game where three friends break into nine rooms together. One player's screen is black, one hears nothing, and one can only send sounds and faces. Each player knows something the others don't, and the only way through is to work out how to tell them.

Most co-op games let the loudest player run the show. Everyone sees the same screen, so one person steers and the others follow.

In Sensory heist nobody has the whole picture. The player who can't see hears the guard's footsteps. The player who can't hear watches the camera's cone. The player who can't speak sees and hears everything but has to say it with a car horn. Teams end up with their own languages: one group agreed an air horn meant "go" and a car horn meant "down".

"I wanted a game where the fun is the talking, not the reflexes," said Aaron Nguyen, who built it. "The best moment in testing was two friends inventing a signal the game never taught them."

To start, open the site, pick a nickname, press Start a new team and send the four-letter code to two friends.

"We laughed through the whole first room. April wandered off and left me in the dark, and then she realised I was relying on her," said Hanna, Canberra.

Sensory heist is free to play in any modern browser today.

> **Is it dull?** No, the hook is strong. But note that every quote and anecdote in it comes from playtest 1, which tested a game that no longer exists. A press release for *this* build has no evidence of its own to quote.

**1. How long does a game take?**
Honestly, we don't know for the current build. Each room has a 4-minute budget and there are nine, plus a reveal before each, so we estimate 40–60 minutes. The last timed human play was an hour for three rooms of an older version. Plan: log `room.clear` times (already recorded, `src/net/attach.ts:341`) and report median time per room. **Test:** one human playtest with a stopwatch, and if it runs past 45 minutes, offer a short game (rooms 1–4).

**2. What do I actually have to do in a room?**
You get everyone onto the exit, with the loot if there is any. We concede the game doesn't say this: the exit's label only appears when you see the exit. Plan: a one-line goal on the reveal and the HUD ("Get all three onto the exit"). **Not now:** a tutorial room 0 (cut in the play overhaul, §2.3), since room 1 already does the job.

**3. Can I play with one friend, or alone?**
Yes. Bots fill empty seats and play from what they perceive. But the fun is people talking, and bots don't talk the way people do. Plan: keep bots as fill, and be honest in the lobby that it's best with three people.

**4. Can I play on my phone?**
Yes: touch controls (a joystick, Act, Wheel) and a 375 px layout, checked by layout specs. The phone has no controls hint, unlike the desktop. Plan: a one-line hint like the desktop's.

**5. Do I need a microphone or headphones?**
No. Callouts, text, faces, clips and marks work without voice. Headphones help in a shared room, and the lobby asks about them. Plan: say "optional" next to the mic line instead of "Microphone blocked".

**6. I'm actually blind / Deaf. Can I play?**
Partly, and we won't claim more. Can't see is built to be playable from sound and captions, with a braille card. But the lobby, the reveal and the menus haven't been tested with a screen reader by a blind player, and Can't hear needs no hearing. **Not now:** a claim of accessibility. **Test:** one session with a blind or low-vision player. Stop claiming "for blind players" anywhere until that happens.

**7. Can I stop after a few rooms?**
You can leave, but there's no "short heist" and no ending until room 9. Plan: a short heist preset (rooms 1–4), if the playtest says 9 is too long.

**8. One Node process on 256 MB. What happens on a crash or a deploy?**
Lobbies and games live in memory (ADR 0009), so a crash or deploy ends every game in progress. Only heist records survive, on `/data`. The README says so. Plan: don't deploy during the crit window, and if a game is lost, the team restarts from room 1. **Not now:** persisting games (it costs much more than it saves at this scale).

**9. Will it scale?**
Not far, and it doesn't need to yet. Voice is relayed through the server (ADR 0010) and is the heaviest load. We estimate tens of lobbies, not hundreds, on a 256 MB machine. `voice.latency` is logged. **Test:** a load script with 10 bot lobbies, watching memory. Stop adding lobbies when RSS passes 200 MB.

**10. How do you know a room is solvable?**
Three perception-only bots clear every room in every rotation (`spec/rooms.test.ts`), and `pnpm lint:rooms` rejects a room where one role holds a station's clue, control and feedback. We concede what they don't prove: bots read view data and have a hand-written decoder per station, so a clue drawn unreadably still passes. And bots can't yet work a cache, so a crew with bots can't open one (`bots-help-caches`, in progress). Plan: a human playtest.

**11. How do you know each role only gets what it should?**
The server sends each role its own view (ADR 0007). `spec/perception.test.ts` reads the raw messages, and `parity.test.ts` has a row for every sound. This is the best-tested promise in the product.

**12. What's voice latency and cost?**
Voice is relayed, not P2P (ADR 0010), and latency is logged as `voice.latency`. We don't have numbers from real use. Plan: read `flyctl logs` after the next playtest.

**13. Is it safe to ship the 45 unmerged commits before Monday?**
Not as they stand. review-fixes-a is in progress because a reviewer found a bug that can hang a room, and review-fixes-b isn't approved. Plan: merge only what's finished and green, behind a full `pnpm check` and a three-tab play of rooms 1–4. **Stop condition:** if fixes-a isn't green by Sunday noon, ship `auto/escape-room` as it is and leave the overnight work for after submission.

**14. Who is this for?**
Small groups of friends, three at a time, who don't play many games (Hanna). The challenge for them is puzzles, chosen because escape-room puzzles are familiar to most people, after environment flips proved too hard. Optional depth (caches, keepsakes) serves enthusiasts on top of that. We're choosing Hanna, which means the first room, the lobby and room 4's puzzle being readable matter more than room 9's cache.

**15. What hasn't been validated?**
Almost everything since 14 October: the comms wheel, line of sight, the station rooms 4–9, caches, braille, and the role balance those were built to fix ("Can't see is deadweight", "Can't speak does their own thing"). These were verified by bots and the author only. **Test:** one human playtest of rooms 1–4 with three people who haven't played. Stop condition: if Can't see is still called "deadweight", the balance rework didn't land.

**16. What was cut?**
Role abilities, drawing and GIFs as channels, a tutorial room 0, a minimap, ambience and music, a periscope, a team codebook, spectators voicing the earpiece, agent-run playtests (on cost, about $55 a heist), and for Monday the diorama look's drawn objects, intro, motion and decor (dl-05..09). See MoSCoW below.

**17. Is a game about disability respectful, and was it made with disabled players?**
The design rule is the Game Accessibility Guidelines' "repeat audio information in text or visuals", and no fact is ever hidden from a role, only routed through a teammate. But no disabled player has been consulted. We concede that's the gap a critic will raise. **Test:** a single consult before any public launch. Not before Monday.

**18. How will we know it's working?**
By teams of three people who finish room 4 together and say they made up a signal. See Goals.

**19. Why nine rooms, not three good ones?**
Each room adds at most two new things (`pacing.test.ts`), which ramps the difficulty well. But nobody has played past room 3 with people. A good short game would beat an untested long one. **Test:** see how far the playtest group gets in 30 minutes.

**20. Why build an automated playtest instead of running a human one?**
The automated plan catches bugs between layers (phase 14 found four), and it's cheap at about $2 a heist. It cannot judge fun, and the PROCESS_LOG says so. Plan: run a human playtest first, and the automated one after submission.

**21. Why not role abilities, like BOMBANANA's braille?**
Abilities prescribe the strategy, and open problem-solving is what makes the talking matter (README). Braille plates came in later as *information* only Can't see reads, not as an ability (ADR 0023). That's a reasonable middle.
