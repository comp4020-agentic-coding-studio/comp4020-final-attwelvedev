## 4. PRFAQ

### Press release

**Sensory heist lets three friends pull off a heist when one can't see, one can't hear and one can't speak**

*A free browser game for three, on laptops or phones. No account, no download. Each player is missing a sense, so the team has to invent its own way of talking.*

CANBERRA, 11 November 2026: Sensory heist is a co-op game where three friends break into nine rooms, from a corridor with two pressure plates to a vault. One player's screen is black. One hears nothing. One can't say a word, only send faces, marks and sounds. Each one knows something the others don't.

Most co-op games let the loudest player run the show. In Sensory heist nobody has the whole picture. The player who can't see reads braille plates. The player who can't hear watches the lights. The player who can't speak hears the safe click.

Teams end up inventing languages the game never taught them. In one playtest, the player who couldn't hear numbered the faces on the comms wheel, counting clockwise from the top. Ten minutes later the player who couldn't see was using those faces to read a braille code across the room.

"I wanted a game where the fun is the talking, not the reflexes," said Aaron Nguyen, who built it.

To start, open the site, pick a nickname, press Start a new team and send the four-letter code to two friends. Bots fill any empty seat.

"We made up a way to send numbers with faces, and by the end we didn't need to think about it," said a playtester in Canberra. (This is paraphrased from playtest 3's notes, not a recorded quote.)

Sensory heist is free to play in any modern browser.

> **Is it dull?** No, and it's better than last time, because its anecdote now comes from the game as it is. Two caveats. "Free to play today" is false until PR 3 deploys. "Nine rooms" is a two-hour promise that the headline hides.

**1. How long does a game take?**
We don't have a clean time. Playtest 3 ran 1.5–2 hours, but it was paused for notes and questions throughout. The upper bound is nine 4-minute budgets plus reveals, about 45 minutes. **Plan:** read the `room.clear` `ms` values from the deployed logs, which gives real play time per room for free. **Not before Wednesday:** a short heist, because it changes the lobby's rotation forecast (ADR 0013) and the leaderboard on deploy day. **Test:** if the median full heist is over 40 minutes, make rooms 1–4 the default.

**2. What do I have to do in a room?**
Get all three onto the exit. The role card says so now. The HUD repeats it only for Can't see, so the sighted lose it once the card closes. **Plan:** the same line in every seat's HUD for room 1 (an hour of work).

**3. Can I play with one friend?**
Yes, and a bot takes the third seat. Playtest 3 was exactly this, and the bot was the weakest player: it can't follow a request, help with a side job in room 7, or understand the signals people invent. **Plan:** say in the lobby that it's best with three people. Make bots obey the wheel's own callouts (Go, Wait, Here). **Not now:** natural-language bots (FAQ 18).

**4. How do I know my teammate has my hand?**
Today you mostly don't. Playtest 3's Can't see player couldn't tell. **Plan:** a spoken and captioned "Bo has your hand" or "Bo let go" for Can't see, and a figure label for the sighted *before* the hold ("Space: hold hands") when in reach. **Test:** in the next playtest, whether Can't see can say, unprompted, who is leading them.

**5. The bot keeps saying "Right". Can I make it stop?**
Yes, in settings, by turning spoken lines off. That's what Hanna did. It shouldn't be needed: bots repeat a moving callout every 1.5 s (`brain.ts:169`). **Plan:** a bot speaks when the direction *changes*, not on a timer.

**6. How do I tell a bot what to do?**
Mostly you can't. A bot solves from what it perceives and what teammates send (ADR 0018), but it treats a human's invented signals as noise. **Plan:** map the wheel's fixed callouts (Here, Go, Wait, Stop, Push) to bot actions, so a person always has a language the bot understands. That's 6 h, after submission.

**7. Can I play on my phone?**
Yes. The phone now has its own hint line (observed). The automated run reports that Move left and right failed on the phone tab in rooms 02 and 03. The backlog says it passes alone and fails only in a full run. **Test:** two minutes on a real phone in room 02. If the stick moves the figure, close it as a harness artifact.

**8. The live site is three weeks old. Why?**
CI deploys only on a push to `main`, and PR 3 is a draft. Everything since 21 October is on `auto/overnight`. **Plan:** merge PR 3 today, let CI deploy, and confirm the live room 1 shows "Get all three onto the exit." on the reveal.

**9. Merge PR 3 now, or keep stacking on its branch?**
Merge now, before the overnight run starts. Four reasons:
- **CI is green right now** on `fadc4eb`, after a day of flake work, and `check:flaky` passed 4 of 4 on the code head. Each commit stacked on top is another chance to go red the night before the deadline.
- **A merge to `main` deploys** (the repo is public, `checks.yml:73`). The marker then meets the reviewed build, even if the overnight run fails.
- **The overnight run becomes a small second PR,** with its own diff, review and CI. Its fixes are easy to judge, and easy to drop if one is bad.
- **It gives the playtest-3 fixes a clean base.** Any regression they cause shows up against a deployed, known-good build, not mixed into 404 commits.

We concede two costs:
- The browser coverage is thinner after the flake work (FAQ 13).
- A deploy ends any game in progress.

**Plan:** merge, let CI deploy, then do a three-tab play of rooms 1–2 on the live URL before starting the overnight run from the new `main`. **Stop condition:** if live room 1 fails, roll back by redeploying `64d17f6`. Games live in memory only, so nothing is lost.

**10. What happens when it crashes or we deploy mid-heist?**
Games live in memory (ADR 0009), so a crash or deploy ends them. Heist records survive on `/data`. **Plan:** no deploys after Wednesday 09:00.

**11. Will it scale?**
To tens of lobbies on 256 MB, not hundreds. Voice relay is the heaviest load. That's enough for a class. **Not now:** load testing.

**12. How do you know a room is solvable, and fair to all three roles?**
Solvable: perception-only bots clear every room in every rotation (`spec/rooms.test.ts`), `lint:rooms` rejects a station whose clue, control and feedback all sit with one role, and `pnpm playtest` now plays every room in three real tabs. Fair: **not proven.** Playtest 3 found the keypad soloable by Can't speak. The lint checks that a role *can't* do it alone, but a role that hears the vent *and* sees the keys can. **Plan:** a lint rule that Can't speak never holds both a station's clue and its control. Redesign the keypad room after submission.

**13. The flaky browser tests were replaced by unit tests. What stopped being checked?**
The labels walk, door pill, restart and reveal browser specs became component and unit tests (`specs/ci-failures-pr3.md`). The doc lists what they no longer check, including a suspected reveal send-while-closed bug that has no test. **Plan:** keep the list, and add a test for that bug only if someone hits it.

**14. How do you know it's correct on a short laptop window or a phone?**
Layout specs run at 1280×800 and 375×812, and nothing shorter. That's how the wheel's tabs vanishing at 560 px got through (observed). **Plan:** add a 1280×600 viewport to the wheel's layout spec, failing first.

**15. Who is this for?**
Small groups of friends who don't play many games, three at a time: Hanna. Playtest 3 was her and the author, and it's the strongest evidence the game works for that pair.

**16. What hasn't been validated?**
Three things:
- A group of three *people* who are new to the game. Playtest 3 had two people, one of them the author, and the other had seen the mechanics.
- The phone in a real session.
- Anything after `32739e8`. The diorama look, intro and decor work came after playtest 3's build, and Hanna's praise of the figures was for the earlier gradient.

**Test:** three newcomers play rooms 1–4 after submission. Stop condition: if any of them can't say what the goal is at 0:30, the HUD goal line moves up the list.

**17. What was cut?**
Natural-language bots, a numbers page, a short heist, a keypad redesign and a signal card, all before Wednesday. The MoSCoW table below gives the reasons. Earlier cuts stand: role abilities, a tutorial room, a minimap, a periscope, a codebook, and agent-run playtests at about $55 a heist.

**18. Why not let bots understand natural language?**
It costs a model call per message on the course proxy and adds a second of latency in a game that needs a reply in seconds. It also pulls against the pitch. The fun Hanna described was two *people* inventing a code, and a bot that understands everything removes the need to invent one. **Instead:** bots obey the wheel's fixed callouts (FAQ 6).

**19. How will we know it's working?**
By teams of three people who clear room 4 together. See Goals. `room.clear` still doesn't log how many bots were in the crew.

**20. Is a game about disability respectful?**
No fact is hidden from a role, only routed through a teammate. The braille is real grade-1, with the number sign leading, as it does in real braille. Playtest 3 noticed that the always-first `#` is "predictable", and that's correct braille, not a flaw. No disabled player has been consulted. **Not before Wednesday:** make no accessibility claims.

**21. Isn't a "numbers" page on the wheel the obvious fix for the face-counting?**
It's obvious, and it would delete the best moment playtest 3 produced. The face-counting is the pitch working. **Not doing it.** If anything, record it: the signal card shows the team its own inventions at the end.
